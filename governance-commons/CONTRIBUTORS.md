Governance Commons. It is referenced by Charter Section 2.4.1 (Solo-
author attestation) to establish current maintainership and to record
the timeline of maintainership changes.

This file is not a complete commit-history audit. Commit history lives
in the repository's version control system. This file records the
identities and roles of substrate-authors at the granularity the
Charter's solo-author attestation mechanism requires.

## Maintainership phase

Per Charter Section 2.4.1, the substrate is in solo-maintainer phase.
Solo-author attestation is the active mechanism for stable lifecycle
promotion. The sunset conditions in Section 2.4.1 are:

- A second maintainer is added to `MAINTENANCE.md` and recorded here
- The substrate version reaches 1.0.0
- The substrate-author of record changes

None of those conditions are currently met. Solo-author attestation
remains the active substitute mechanism.

## Roles

The substrate currently recognizes one role. Future role distinctions
may be introduced under Charter Article IX amendment when the
substrate's maintainership matures.

- **substrate-author**: holds authority under Charter Article II
  (authoring discipline), Article IV (catalog identity), and Article
  VIII (lifecycle management). Authors and attests content. In
  solo-author phase, the substrate-author is also the attester per
  Section 2.4.1.

## How attestations reference this file

When a substrate artifact is promoted to stable lifecycle status under
Section 2.4.1, the artifact's provenance records the reviewer as
`<identity>-self-attested` (for example `myoung-self-attested`). The
suffix marks the attestation as solo-author rather than as
second-human review. The unsuffixed identity (here `myoung`) is the
identity recorded in this file.

The convention exists so that auditors and downstream consumers can
distinguish content reviewed by a second human from content attested
under Section 2.4.1. The distinction is recorded honestly rather than
obscured.
